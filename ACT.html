What Is the Enhanced ACT?

The ACT is a curriculum-based college admissions exam accepted by all US universities and many institutions worldwide. Scores range from 1–36. The Enhanced ACT is now the only version offered: three required sections (English, Math and Reading) that take about 2 hours 5 minutes, plus optional Science and Writing sections.

English

50 Questions · 35 Minutes

Tests grammar, punctuation, sentence structure, and rhetorical skills. It's the fastest-paced section — about 42 seconds per question — so speed and precision both matter.

Mathematics

45 Questions · 50 Minutes

Covers pre-algebra through basic trigonometry and statistics, now with four answer choices instead of five. A calculator is allowed throughout, and difficulty still escalates toward the end.

Reading

36 Questions · 40 Minutes

Passages drawn from literary narrative and informational texts. You get a little more time per question than before (about 67 seconds), but passage management is still critical.

Science (Optional)

40 Questions · 40 Minutes

Tests data interpretation and reasoning — not memorized science facts. It no longer counts toward your composite; if you take it you receive a separate Science score and a STEM score.

What Changed on the ACT?

The ACT was shortened and redesigned, with online testing rolled out nationally in April 2025. The content is familiar, but the pacing, scoring and strategy are different — so prep built for the old test no longer fits.

ACT Tutoring FAQ

What changed on the ACT, and does it affect how I should prepare?
Yes. The Enhanced ACT is shorter (about 2 hours 5 minutes for the required sections), English is 50 questions in 35 minutes, Math is 45 questions in 50 minutes with four answer choices, Reading is 36 questions in 40 minutes, and Science is optional. Your composite is now the average of English, Math and Reading only. Practice tests and strategies built for the old format no longer match, so Neil's prep is built around the new one.
Should I take the optional ACT Science section?
Science can't lower your composite, because it's scored separately and reported with a STEM score (the average of Math and Science). It's worth taking if you're applying to STEM programs or a school that asks for a Science or STEM score — and you don't need to know any science content, since it tests data interpretation. If your target schools don't require it, skipping it saves about 40 minutes and lets you focus on the three scored sections. Neil can help you decide after a short diagnostic.
Does superscoring still work?
For the Enhanced ACT, the Superscore Composite is calculated from your highest English, Math and Reading scores across all your attempts. Science is not included in the Superscore Composite, although it still feeds the separate STEM score. Whether a school superscores at all is that school's policy, so check each one.
Is the ACT harder than the SAT?
Neither is objectively harder — they test different things in different ways. The ACT moves faster (especially English) and offers an optional Science section. The SAT uses an adaptive digital format and goes deeper on algebra and data analysis. Most students have a natural fit for one or the other, which Neil can help identify through a short diagnostic.
Should I take the ACT online or on paper?
Both versions cover the same Enhanced ACT content, and you choose when you register. Online testing became available nationally in April 2025, and paper is still offered. Switching between the two after registering can cost extra, so decide first. Neil prepares students for whichever format they pick, including practice in that format.
When are the next ACT test dates?
The remaining 2026–27 national dates are December 12, 2026, then February 27, April 10, June 12 and July 10, 2027 (the September and October 2026 dates have passed). Registration deadlines fall about four to five weeks before each test, and seats can fill, so register early at act.org and confirm dates there. Neil recommends starting prep at least 8–12 weeks before your target date.
How many sessions will I need?
Most students see meaningful improvement within 8–12 weeks of consistent sessions. Students aiming for a 3–5 point gain often need fewer sessions than those targeting a 7–10 point jump. After the diagnostic, Neil gives you a realistic, honest timeline with no pressure to commit to a large package.
Do you tutor in-person or online?
Both. Neil tutors students in-person in Toronto and the GTA, and online worldwide via video call. The approach and results are equally strong in both formats.
Can I take both the SAT and ACT?
Yes, and some students do. However, most students get better results by focusing their prep on one exam. After a diagnostic session Neil can advise which test is the better strategic choice for your specific profile and university targets.
Do you also help with SAT, GRE, and GMAT?
Yes. In addition to the ACT, Neil tutors the SAT (averaging 100–150 point gains), GRE, and GMAT (averaging 70–100 point improvements). All four tests can be discussed during the free consultation.
